<section id="prerequisites">
  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold mt-6 mb-4">Prerequisites: What You Must Have Ready Before Logging In</h2>
  <div className="alert alert-warning bg-red-50 p-4 rounded-md mb-4 border-l-4 border-red-500">
    <strong>Crucial Step:</strong> Do not open the CET Cell portal until you have completed your offline homework. The portal has a session timeout, and trying to research colleges while the timer ticks down is a recipe for disaster.
  </div>
  
  <p>Before you begin, ensure you have:</p>
  <ol className="list-decimal pl-6 space-y-2">
    <li><strong>Your Final Merit Number:</strong> Your percentile doesn't dictate your admission; your State General Merit Number (and Category Merit Number, if applicable) does.</li>
    <li><strong>The Final Seat Matrix:</strong> This document, released by the CET cell just before the option form opens, shows exactly how many seats are available in each branch, across every category, for every college.</li>
    <li><strong>A Physical "Master List" of Choice Codes:</strong> Every college branch has a unique 9-digit choice code (e.g., <code>600624510</code> for COEP Computer Engineering). You need to write these down on a piece of paper or an Excel sheet, arranged strictly in your order of preference.</li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section id="step-by-step-process">
  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold mt-6 mb-4">Step-by-Step Process to Fill the CAP Option Form</h2>

  <h3 className="text-xl font-semibold mt-4 mb-2">Step 1: Login and Access the Option Form</h3>
  <p>
    Head to the official State CET Cell Maharashtra portal. Log in using your Application ID and Password. On your dashboard, you will see a flashing link that says <strong>"Fill Option Form for CAP Round-I."</strong> Click it. The system will first ask you to read a set of instructions. Read them carefully, check the declaration box, and proceed.
  </p>

  <h3 className="text-xl font-semibold mt-4 mb-2">Step 2: Filtering by Universities and Districts</h3>
  <p>The system will present you with a screen to filter colleges. You do not have to search blindly.</p>
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-1 mb-4">
    <li><strong>Select Course:</strong> Choose the branches you are interested in (e.g., Computer Engineering, Information Technology, Artificial Intelligence and Data Science).</li>
    <li><strong>Select University:</strong> If you specifically want Savitribai Phule Pune University (SPPU) or Mumbai University (MU), check those boxes.</li>
    <li><strong>Select District:</strong> You can narrow down choices to specific districts like Pune, Mumbai Suburban, or Nagpur.</li>
    <li><strong>Select Institute Status:</strong> Choose between Government, Government-Aided, University Departments, and Un-Aided (Private) colleges.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Once you hit "Search," the portal will generate a massive list of all colleges fitting your criteria.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl font-semibold mt-4 mb-2">Step 3: Selecting Your Institutes</h3>
  <p>
    This is the "Cart" stage. You are not ranking the colleges yet; you are simply adding them to your potential list. Scroll through the generated list and check the boxes next to the colleges and branches you are interested in. <strong>Be extremely careful here.</strong> Many colleges have similar names but are located in entirely different areas. Always verify the 4-digit DTE Institute Code (e.g., <code>6289</code> vs. <code>6122</code>).
  </p>
  <p>Once you have selected all your desired colleges, click <button className="btn-primary text-blue-600 font-bold border rounded px-2 py-1 bg-gray-100">"Add Selected Options"</button>.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl font-semibold mt-4 mb-2">Step 4: Prioritizing Your Options (The Most Critical Step)</h3>
  <p>This is where admissions are won and lost. The screen will now show all the colleges you selected in Step 3. You must now assign a preference number (1, 2, 3, etc.) to each choice code.</p>
  <div className="bg-gray-50 p-4 border rounded-md my-4">
    <h4 className="font-bold mb-2">The Professional Counselor Strategy:</h4>
    <ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2">
      <li><strong>Preferences 1 to 5 (The Dream Colleges):</strong> These should be the absolute top-tier colleges (like VJTI, COEP, SPIT, PICT) that have cutoffs slightly higher than your current merit rank. Never assume you won't get in; cutoffs can drop unexpectedly.</li>
      <li><strong>Preferences 6 to 15 (The Target Colleges):</strong> These are the colleges where last year's cutoff exactly matches your current merit rank. This is where you are mathematically most likely to land. Order these strictly by which campus you like the most, not by their cutoff numbers.</li>
      <li><strong>Preferences 16 to 25 (The Safe Colleges):</strong> These are colleges where the cutoff is significantly lower than your rank. You must include these. If the cutoffs skyrocket this year, these safe options guarantee you do not get pushed out of the CAP rounds entirely.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <p>Use the up and down arrows provided on the portal to shift the colleges into your perfect sequence.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl font-semibold mt-4 mb-2">Step 5: Reviewing and Confirming</h3>
  <p>
    Once your list is ordered, proceed to the summary page. The system will display your final list of choice codes in numerical order. <strong>Print this page or save it as a PDF before confirming.</strong> Double-check every single 9-digit code against your original offline list.
  </p>
  <p>
    To finally submit, you will need to enter your login password and an OTP sent to your registered mobile number. Once you enter the OTP, your form is locked. <strong>You cannot change it until the next CAP round.</strong>
  </p>
</section>

<hr className="my-8 border-gray-300" />

<section id="auto-freeze-rule">
  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold mt-6 mb-4 text-red-600">The Golden Rule: How Option #1 Works (Auto-Freeze)</h2>
  <p className="text-lg font-medium">
    I cannot stress this enough: <strong>The college you put at Preference Number 1 is subject to the Auto-Freeze rule.</strong>
  </p>
  <p>
    If the CET algorithm allocates you the college you listed as your 1st preference, your seat is automatically frozen. You have zero choice in the matter. You must pay the seat acceptance fee and report to that college to take admission. If you fail to do so, you are entirely thrown out of the CAP process for the subsequent rounds.
  </p>
  <p>
    Therefore, <strong>Option #1 must be your absolute dream college.</strong> Do not put a "safe" college at number 1 just because you want to see an allotment in Round 1. If you get it, you are trapped.
  </p>
  <p>
    If you are allotted any preference from number 2 downwards, you have the option to "Betterment" (Float), meaning you can hold that seat while trying for a better one in Round 2.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="tfws-explained">
  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold mt-6 mb-4">TFWS Explained: Should You Opt for It?</h2>
  <p>
    The Tuition Fee Waiver Scheme (TFWS) is a massive financial relief mechanism for students whose family income is below ₹8 Lakhs per annum. Under TFWS, 5% supernumerary (extra) seats are created in every branch, and the <strong>entire tuition fee is waived for all four years of engineering.</strong>
  </p>
  <p>
    When you fill the option form, TFWS seats have a separate choice code ending with the letter <strong>'T'</strong> (e.g., <code>600624510T</code>).
  </p>

  <h3 className="text-xl font-semibold mt-4 mb-2">How to place TFWS codes in your form:</h3>
  <p>If you want to try for a TFWS seat in a college, always place the 'T' code immediately <em>above</em> the regular choice code for that exact same branch.</p>
  <ul className="list-none pl-6 space-y-1 mb-6 bg-blue-50 p-4 rounded border-l-4 border-blue-500">

<section id="top-mistakes">
  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold mt-6 mb-4">Top 3 Mistakes Students Make While Filling the Form</h2>
  <p>Even with guides like this, students panic and make critical errors. Avoid these three at all costs:</p>
  <ol className="list-decimal pl-6 space-y-3">
    <li><strong>Judging purely by last year's cutoff:</strong> Cutoffs are a reference, not a rule. If a new AI/DS branch was introduced last year, its cutoff will likely jump significantly this year as it becomes more established. Always build a buffer.</li>
    <li><strong>Filling only 5 options:</strong> The portal allows you to fill up to 300 options. While you don't need 300, filling only 4 or 5 options is incredibly risky unless you have a 99.9 percentile. Aim for a balanced list of at least 30 to 40 realistic options.</li>
    <li><strong>Ignoring the Branch vs. College dilemma:</strong> Don't put Civil Engineering at a top college higher than Computer Science at a good college if you ultimately want to be a software developer. Do not compromise your long-term career purely for a college brand name.</li>
  </ol>
</section>
